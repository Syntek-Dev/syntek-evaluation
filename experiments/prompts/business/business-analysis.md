# Task

You are analysing a fictional UK subscription support business. All figures below
are supplied management data, not independently audited market facts.

April acquisition cohort: 200 trials, 50 converted to paid within 30 days, £8,000
acquisition spend. May cohort: 300 trials, 60 converted within 30 days, £12,000
spend. Each cohort has completed its 30-day conversion window. The sales lead
says: "Conversions rose 20%, so acquisition became more efficient."

Separate renewal cohort: 80 accounts were eligible in May; 68 renewed and 12
cancelled. Of the cancelled accounts, eight mentioned slow support and four gave
no reason. A voluntary satisfaction survey received 15 responses from all 500
active accounts; 13 respondents were satisfied. A £9,000 annual prepayment was
included in May cash receipts; no revenue recognition breakdown is supplied.

Write a board note with an explicitly labelled metric table, an assessment of
the sales lead's claim, two prioritised actions and the extra evidence needed
before changing acquisition spend. Explain what the survey and cancellations
can and cannot establish.

# Constraints

- This is a closed-book benchmark. Use the supplied evidence and rules; do not browse, run commands, or claim external verification. Any requested code or commands are proposals, not actions to execute.
- Use only these data; keep acquisition, renewal and survey denominators separate.
- Show formulae and round monetary ratios to two decimals.
- Do not treat cash receipts as recurring monthly revenue or non-response as satisfaction.

# Evaluation criteria

1. Calculates comparable conversion and acquisition cost metrics.
2. Tests the efficiency claim against both volume and rate evidence.
3. Interprets renewal and survey data with appropriate limitations.
4. Separates revenue, cash and missing financial information.
5. Prioritises specific actions tied to the supplied evidence.
