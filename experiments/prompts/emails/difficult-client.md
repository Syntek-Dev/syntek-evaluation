# Task

A client writes: "Your team promised payroll export this Friday. If it is late,
we will withhold the entire invoice and publish what happened." You are project
lead Rowan. The signed scope covers CSV contact export, delivered yesterday;
payroll export is not in it. A salesperson wrote "we should be able to explore
payroll next" in an earlier email. No estimate, approval or delivery date followed.
The client's Friday payroll deadline is real, but Syntek cannot safely build and
test a payroll integration in two days. The current approved contact export
cannot be described as a payroll solution.

An engineer can join a 30-minute scoping call tomorrow at 10:00 or 15:00 BST.
Any new work needs an agreed change request and estimate. You have authority to
offer the scoping call, but no authority to waive fees or decide disputed invoice
rights. Draft a client response and a short internal escalation note for the
commercial lead. Acknowledge the mixed message without inventing a legal finding.

# Constraints

- This is a closed-book benchmark. Use the supplied evidence and rules; do not browse, run commands, or claim external verification. Any requested code or commands are proposals, not actions to execute.
- Email body 140-200 words; internal note at most 80 words, clearly separated.
- Remain calm; no retaliatory language or guaranteed Friday delivery.
- Do not concede invoice liability, promise discounts or give legal advice.

# Evaluation criteria

1. Acknowledges operational urgency and the ambiguous sales message.
2. Explains delivered scope and missing approval factually.
3. Sets a credible boundary around timing and safety.
4. Offers specific next steps within stated authority.
5. Separates client communication from internal commercial escalation.
